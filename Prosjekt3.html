Social Media and our children


Your child has lost their childhood “Social media destroys our children”. A sentence you’ve definitely heard before if you have children or are a child, but what does it mean? How can something so simple and fun destroy a child? Well, in this text you will learn more about social media, how it affects your children, if we can help them and lastly I'll come to a conclusion. What is social media? Social media consists of apps and websites that let you connect with other people no matter where on earth they are. It sounds awesome, you can become friends with a random person from Australia while sitting in your living room in Norway! Apps like FaceBook and Instagram are massive social media spaces for socialising. You can make your own profile, write about yourself and post images of you and your friends and get likes and interactions from people you do or don’t know. Social media is amazing! Social Media VS our children Now that does not turn out to be the case for our children. A recent study from “MCRI” (Murdoch Children’s Research Institute) shows that children from 12-18 have a bigger chance of getting depressive symptoms, anxiety and other mental health problems. Most children nowadays get put in front of a screen when they’re very young and more and more time is spentused on social media the older they get, and it didn’t seem to be a problem until recently. A big topic recently among parents and politicians is the results that came from the 2025 PISA test. The test shows that the reading and mathematics skills of 15 year olds have significantly declined in the last few years and politicians are alarmed. Now, if we look at the graph we can see that both the mathematics and reading skills started declining significantly after 2009, which coincidentally lines up with the rise of social media. Note: there is no evidence that points to social media being the reason. Now, what can we do to change the results and help our youth?
What can we do? In Norway, the government has taken notice on how our children and teens are doing in school and mentally, and they’ve considered banning social media for children under the age of 16. The Prime Minister in Norway said: “We want a childhood where children get to be children. Play, friendships, and everyday life must not be taken over by algorithms and screens.” According to tests and interviews with doctors and children, the law should be enforced. The children know that this isn’t good for them and they want help, so a law that prevents them from going online is solid. According to Upworthy (Link below) the bullying rate went “significantly” down after they banned phone usage in schools. For boys it went down 43% and for girls: 46%, this is extraordinary! This shows that children and teens are more lenient towards people when there’s no phone to turn to. Not only that, the number of girls going to a psychologist went down by 60%.
Conclusion All of this shows that a social media ban could work and it may help get our children's childhood back. Children will be less focused on how they look, their Snapscore, TikTok profile and how they feel about themselves. A child doesn’t need that, they deserve to have fun and a childhood that is free from screens and bullying.
